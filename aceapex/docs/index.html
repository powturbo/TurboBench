Random-Access Compressed VRAM Layer

Seek any tile in compressed data — with a provable bound on decode depth.

Don't decompress 50 GB to read 16 KB. Any tile decodes in 0.27 ms — from the start, middle, or end of the archive. One file, both CPU and GPU. Back-references are absolute output positions, so every block stands alone.

Where ACEAPEX loses — stated first, in our own words

A comparison built only from wins invites the reviewer to fill the gaps in public. These are ours to name.

zstd-19 packs tighter — when it is allowed a whole-file window
On the same genome: ratio 4.04 (ours) vs 4.93 (zstd-19). Ratio is not our axis. But the comparison only means something once the constraint is equalised: under independent 16 KB blocks, where every codec is equally seekable, ACEAPEX is first on 3 of 5 corpora against zstd-19 (enwik8 2.638, enwik9 2.981, silesia 3.005) and ahead of zstd-3 everywhere by 4.9–16.1%. Blocking itself costs 6.7% on genome and 24.2% on text; the match layer returns about three quarters of it. See Paper 5.
A single cold tile over PCIe is faster
~10 µs for a 16 KB PCIe fetch vs 272 µs for one seek. The win is in aggregate and in residency — never staging the 50 GB at all.
Peak throughput belongs to hardware
Fixed-function decompression engines and DietGPU reach ~600 GB/s. Our 200 GB/s buys seekability, not raw rate.
